# Desert ants (*Cataglyphis*): a third species with different senses and signals

*Research note for the species epic (PRD §60). Written 2026-11-11, against code at `01e39f4`. **Read in full text:** Razin et al. 2013 (§1), Dillier & Wehner 2004 (§5), Buehlmann et al. 2012 and 2013 (§3; the 2012 paper through the author's thesis reprint). **Not read in full:** Wehner, Marsh & Wehner 1992 (abstract only) and Schmid-Hempel & Schmid-Hempel 1984 (a summary), both paywalled; Buehlmann et al. 2014 (only the pre-publication thesis draft). Everything else comes from search summaries or abstracts and is marked **unverified**, as in [nest-search](nest-search.md) and [forager-trips](forager-trips.md). Numbers recalled without a fetched source are marked "(from memory; not fetched)". Before a number here is built on, read the paper.*

The game has two colonies of one generic mound ant (trail-laying recruiters) and the player's fungus. The owner wants a third kind of agent whose senses and signals differ, and chose the desert ant first. The question this note answers: **what does a solitary desert-ant forager sense and signal, how does its colony live, and which of that can the game's one-policy shape carry without a scripted choice?** The answer also fixes what the species descriptor ([refactor.md](refactor.md) S4.3, `ja-fnd.5.3`) must be able to express: a species with *fewer* channels, a different forager career and different senses, not only one more channel.

## 1. Foragers work alone: no trail, no recruitment to a place

- *Cataglyphis* (and the related *Ocymyrmex*, *Melophorus*) forage solitarily and were long thought to have no chemical recruitment. Efforts to elicit recruitment to rich, renewable feeders in several *Cataglyphis* species never produced cooperative behaviour. A hypothesis offered is that volatile pheromones fail at desert surface temperatures. Sources: Sommer et al. 2013 (on *Ocymyrmex robustior*; journal unconfirmed); Lenoir et al. 2009 (review, *Israel J Entomol*). **Unverified**, snippet-level.
- Pheromone trails are apparently absent. Reasons given: scattered, unpredictable food, short trail life at desert temperatures, and path integration doing the trail's job. Source: Wolf & Wehner 2000 (*J Exp Biol* 203:857). **Unverified.**
- What exists is weak and non-spatial. **Read in full text** (Razin, Eckmann & Feinerman 2013, *J R Soc Interface* 10:20130079, doi:10.1098/rsif.2013.0079, [PMC3627090](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3627090/); *C. niger*, four lab colonies of a few hundred ants from Rehovot, queenless, groups of 2–13 ants in an entrance chamber, an immobilised cricket in the arena): the paper opens with *C. niger* as "a species that lacks pheromone-based recruitment mechanisms". The ant that finds food (the "recruiter") runs between food and nest at high speed. Inside the nest an ant that meets a faster ant tends to speed up, and one that meets a slower one tends to slow down, with a crossing at about 2 cm/s, far below the exit threshold. An ant's chance of leaving the nest rises with its own speed with a soft threshold at about **8 cm/s**, and *given its speed* it does not depend on whether it met a recruiter in the last 2 minutes (p = 0.46). A recruiter's entry raises the number of idle ants that leave in the next minute **sevenfold** over a spontaneous mover (n = 229 events, nests of 4–8 ants). Higher ant density in the nest **lowers** the exit rate and recruitment success. Recruits get no information about where the food is. Earlier, an "invitation" display (Szlep 1973, cited in a review, **unverified**).
- Contrary evidence: the related *Ocymyrmex robustior* does group-recruit (a leader takes about 2–7 recruits to patches too big for one ant, Sommer et al. 2013). So "desert ant" is not uniformly solitary, and "no recruitment" is a statement about *Cataglyphis*, not the climate. No tandem running or group recruitment was found for any *Cataglyphis* species, including *C. cursor* and *C. floricola* (searched for, not found).

**In the game:** the species has **no food trail, no home trail, no raid trail**: its channel set is smaller than the generic ant's. The mode choice for an empty-jawed ant therefore cannot read `foodTrail` (always "none"). The finder's invitation is **speed**: the ant's own body state (its speed; sense-table row: own body) is passed on by contact (encounter rate row), and the chance to leave rises with own speed past a soft threshold. That uses the existing `leave` response, not a new channel, and is a measured rule: a returning finder raises idle nestmates' speed, they go out, and a crowded nest slows exit (negative feedback). Built, because the fungus's contact lever on this host needs it (owner, 2026-11-11). **Design:** the game has no per-ant speed state yet, so a nest-speed variable per ant is a body rule (like fatigue), with rates fitted to the figures above (threshold 8 cm/s, crossing about 2 cm/s, sevenfold); the lab values are for a few-ant chamber, so scaling to a nest is an assumption.

## 2. They navigate by path integration, a sky compass and remembered views

- The odometer is a stride integrator: *C. fortis* given stilts overshot and given stumps undershot their home distance (Wittlinger, Wehner & Wolf 2006, *Science* 312:1965). The undershoot grows with distance (Sommer & Wehner 2004, *J Comp Physiol A* 190:1). **Unverified**, abstract-level.
- Optic flow: one study found walking ants do not rely on self-induced flow for distance; later work (Wolf, Wittlinger & Pfeffer 2018, journal unconfirmed) reports two odometers, stride and optic flow, with separate memories. **Unverified; unresolved.**
- The compass is the sky's polarisation pattern read by UV receptors in the dorsal rim of the eye (Labhart 2000, *Naturwissenschaften*). About 100 rim ommatidia per eye and a 1–2° compass error are **from memory; not fetched**.
- Foraging runs: GPS tracks of *C. fortis* on a salt pan reach up to 1,259 m (median 341 m) with a maximum of 356 m from the nest, and 53 % of foragers found food (Buehlmann et al. 2014, from the author's thesis draft, **unverified against the published paper**). Mean maximum foraging distance 28 m and mean distance to the nearest nest 23 m in the 2012 field data (Buehlmann et al. 2012, thesis reprint, full text read). Earlier abstract-level figures: 32–1,238 m ([forager-trips](forager-trips.md)).
- Error is small near home and grows with the path walked; the search when the vector runs out is systematic and widens with uncertainty (Müller & Wehner 1994; Merkle, Knaden & Wehner 2006, *J Exp Biol* 209:3545): already the game's model ([nest-search](nest-search.md)).
- Learning walks: naive *C. fortis* make 3–7 widening walks near the entrance before foraging (Fleischmann et al. 2016, *J Exp Biol*, **unverified**); on featureless salt pans they make "voltes" without stopping to look back at the nest, while species in cluttered habitat make stop-and-look pirouettes (Fleischmann et al. 2017, **unverified**). A geomagnetic cue aligns the gaze in naive ants (Fleischmann et al. 2018, *Curr Biol*, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2018.03.043), but later work did not replicate that for re-learning walks. **Unverified, contested.**
- Walking speed up to about 1 m/s (50–100 body lengths a second) is **from memory; not fetched**. [ant-communication §2](ant-communication.md#2-distance-and-time-scales) already carries the ratio.

**In the game:** the navigation senses are already in the sense table (path integration, route memory *design*, landmarks and entrance odour); the desert ant **leans on them and has little else**. That is a *weighting*, not a new sense: the species trusts its vector and route memory and has no trail to override them. Route memory is not built (`ja-lp4.16`, [route-memory](route-memory.md)); the species would make it matter, so that bead moves from optional to a dependency (see The mechanic). **Design:** the compass is not modelled (the plane has no sky); the compass error stays inside the existing 10 % vector drift.

## 3. They find food by smell, not by following one another

- *C. fortis* finds food by smell: dead-cricket-on-a-stick followed at under 2 m by 79 % of ants (n = 38), at 2–4 m by 53 % (n = 40), at over 4 m by 40 % (n = 15), by 0 % of controls; the longest turn-in-and-retrieve was 5.9 m. A 5-mm cricket 5–300 m from the nest was found in a median 2.4–2.9 min. 6 of 15 odours were attractive; linoleic acid (the dead-arthropod smell) worked down to a 10⁻⁵ dilution (solvent and units not seen). Crosswind runs are described only qualitatively; wind speed, running speed and run width were not retrieved. Source: Buehlmann, Graham, Hansson & Knaden 2014, *Curr Biol* 24(9):960–964, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2014.02.056, **numbers from the thesis draft, not the published text: check each**. In the 2013 paper (full text read) 29 of 30 ants followed a food plume 18 m before the expected feeder, and a single-cricket plume drew 25 of 27 ants trained on crickets (Buehlmann et al. 2013, *Biol Lett* 9:20130070).
- On the way out, odour outweighs the path-integration vector near food, and ants learn odours along the way home and use them when other cues fail (Buehlmann et al. 2013, *Biol Lett* 9:20130070, full text read; the second claim, Buehlmann et al. 2015, is **unverified**).
- Homing *C. fortis* run up the nest's own CO₂ plume, but only when the integrator says they are near home (Buehlmann, Hansson & Knaden 2012, *Curr Biol* 22:645; **full text read in the author's thesis**). Ants turned straight to the nest when passing within about **0.3 m** downwind (the assumed plume width). CO₂ 2 cm downwind of the opening: median 352 ppm at nests (n = 18) against 279 ppm at controls (n = 13); the artificial plume was about 500 ppm, higher than the nest's, and the paper does not reconcile this. 71 % followed their own nest's plume, 73 % a foreign nest's, 62 % the CO₂ plume, 16 % in the no-nest control. Entering a foreign nest killed 13 of 27 displaced ants (48 %; dead = not seen within 2 h). Wind speed (only direction), running speed and crosswind width are not reported.
- Diet is dead arthropods, found alone, at the hottest hours, with occasional small live prey and extrafloral nectar (a review; **unverified**). *C. floricola* uses petals. Food storage, honeydew, cannibalism and brood feeding with prey: **not found**; do not assume granaries (those appeared for *Pogonomyrmex* and *Messor* only).
- Per-trip numbers: *C. bicolor* trips lasted about 23 min early in a forager's career and about 62 min a week later, and finds rose from 25 % to 80 % of runs (Schmid-Hempel & Schmid-Hempel 1984, a summary read, **body paywalled**). An earlier snippet gave about 4.2 trips a day with a run of about 52.9 min in a channel experiment (paper unnamed; **unverified**). Load size, trip success in the field and food sharing at the nest: **not found.**

**In the game:** the new **sense is food odour in the air** (a plume the ant crosses and follows upwind), a new row in the sense table: range several metres (many cells), no memory, a gradient. It is a *steering* cue (free: no situation field), like the existing nest-odour search ([nest-search](nest-search.md)), but its source is the food itself, not a laid trail. **Design:** the plume's shape and the crosswind run; the papers give "several metres" only. Without a wind field the first build uses a radial gradient from each food item (not a laid channel): the food patch itself is the source, so no ant-laid trail exists. This is the one new world field; the radial gradient first is decided (Open questions 3).

## 4. Heat sets when and how long they can work

- *C. bombycina*: the foraging window is squeezed between predation (below it) and heat stress (above it), and foragers leave in "an explosive outburst confined to a few minutes per day" at the hottest hours (Wehner, Marsh & Wehner 1992, *Nature* 357:586, doi:10.1038/357586a0; **the abstract was read, the letter is paywalled**). Secondary sources, **not checked against the letter**: a window of 46.5–53.6 °C whose lower limit is set by the lizard *Acanthodactylus dumerili* retreating underground (it is not clear whether this is body, surface or ant-height temperature, and the upper figure equals the 1995 CTmax below, so two papers may be merged); ants stay in burrows until the temperature at ant height (4 mm) reaches 45–47 °C; and about 75 % of foraging time on the hottest days is spent on thermal refuges (dry stems). Burst length in minutes and how fast released ants were killed: not retrieved.
- Critical thermal maxima of 53.6 ± 0.8 °C (*C. bombycina*) and 55.1 ± 1.1 °C (*C. bicolor*) are from Gehring & Wehner 1995 (*PNAS*), **not the 1992 letter**, read through papers that cite them (**unverified**); one later paper gives 53.6 °C over 10 min of exposure. Surface temperatures reach about 70 °C and the air 4 mm up is 6–7 °C cooler (secondary summaries that conflict on the reference point; **unverified**).
- On the hottest days (about 45 °C) *C. fortis* in a channel experiment died of desiccation in about 2 h (paper unnamed; **unverified**). A hair coat cools *C. bombycina* by 5–10 °C (a 2020 *Science* paper; authors not captured; **unverified**). **These heat figures are for *C. bombycina* and *C. bicolor*, not *C. fortis*, the species the game would follow: parameters for it are a design choice.**
- *C. bicolor* in Tunisia (Schmid-Hempel & Schmid-Hempel 1984, a summary read, **body paywalled**): marked foragers lost a constant **16.4 % a day**, giving a quoted half-life of 4.2 days and a life expectancy of 6.1 days (these two do not agree: 16.4 % a day gives a half-life of about 3.9 days, so pick one set). The main nest had about 290 foragers and the number stayed stable. Losses are attributed mainly to predation by spiders and robber flies, not counted directly. Callows introduced into the nest stayed about four weeks (10–43 days) before foraging. A further estimate: about 6 days forager lifetime (Kühn-Bühlmann & Wehner 2006, doi:10.1002/neu.20235, **not read**). Humidity cues: **not found.** The "about 6 days" often quoted for *C. fortis* is almost certainly the *C. bicolor* figure.

**In the game:** heat is a **world field and a body rule**, not a choice: a ground temperature that varies over the day (and by shade) and an ant's **own body heat** that rises while it is out in the open and falls in shade or underground. The ant **senses its own heat** (a sense-table row: own body, which already covers hunger and fatigue); a policy over that sense decides to return, stop or press on. The death at a critical heat is physiology, the same kind of body rule as the fungus's. **Design:** the whole heat model (curve of day heat, rates of gain and cooling); the literature gives windows and critical points, not rates. This is the species' own pressure, and it is why it forages in bursts.

## 5. The colony: small, usually one queen, founding by budding on foot, aggressive to strangers

- *C. cursor*: a one-queen colony makes about 96.4 % of daughter queens by thelytokous parthenogenesis and about 97.9 % of daughter workers from fertilised eggs; a worker-produced queen heads more than 60 % of colonies (Pearcy et al. 2004, *Science*; Pearcy, Hardy & Aron 2006, *Heredity*; **unverified**). Founding is by fission on foot: young queens leave with workers and found about 4 nests at about 7 m on average (Lenoir et al. 1988 and Chéron et al. 2011, quoted in a *Heredity* article; **unverified, second-hand**). Not general: *C. cursor* only.
- **Read in full text** (Dillier & Wehner 2004, *Insectes Sociaux* 51:186–196, doi:10.1007/s00040-003-0722-0, from the Wehner lab's PDF; densities in the means below are my unit conversion). Both species are monogynous (one egg-laying queen per colony). *C. fortis* is monodomous and *C. bicolor* polydomous, and they differ a lot: colony size 202 ± 125 workers (N = 4) against 3,630 ± 2,442 (N = 8); nest density about 3 a hectare (Table 1: 2.6–3.5) against about 15 (Table 2: 11–22); nearest-neighbour nest distance 40.6 m against 9.1 m (colonies 29–39 m apart); worker mass 8.8 against 23.0 mg. *C. bicolor* runs 3.2 active nests at a time (max 11), 6.1 over a season (max 15). Nest-site survival from year to year is 76–84 % for *C. fortis* (at least 9.4 % of 340 nests lasted 9 or more years) and 4.9 % for *C. bicolor* (N = 139): **this is nest-site persistence, not colony death**, because *C. bicolor* colonies move. A review's "less than a few hundred workers" holds for *C. fortis* only.
- Nest depth, entrances per nest and chambers are **not reported** in Dillier & Wehner (they map entrances only) and were not found elsewhere. Colony lifespan, sex ratio, nuptial flights: **not found.** Founding mode for *C. fortis*: not found.
- Between-colony aggression in *C. iberica* is always at maximum, whatever the distance, and tracks the colony-specific cuticular-hydrocarbon profile (Dahbi, Cerdá, Hefetz & Lenoir 1996, *J Chem Ecol*; **unverified**); a colony odour and a queen-specific odour coexist (Dahbi & Lenoir 1998, *Insectes Sociaux*). *C. niger* aggression and hydrocarbons match its genetic structure, with a "nasty neighbour" effect (Saar et al. 2014, *Front Ecol Evol* 2:13, doi:10.3389/fevo.2014.00013; **unverified**).
- Workers start foraging after about four weeks inside (Schmid-Hempel 1984, as above); foragers have higher phenoloxidase activity than workers inside (*C. velox* and *C. cursor*, a title seen; **unverified**), read as up-regulated defence against infection and wounds outside. Dillier & Wehner say there is no evidence yet of a disease or parasite function for polydomy in their populations.
- Forager fraction of the colony, interior worker lifespan, brood care: **not found.**

**In the game:** the colony follows the generic ant's structure with different **parameters** (small cap, one queen, short forager career, one nest) and the existing recognition odour and tolerance ([ant-communication §3](ant-communication.md#3-rival-colonies)). Between *Cataglyphis* colonies tolerance stays at its stranger setting: aggression is the baseline. **Decided (proposed, 2026-11-12): the game's desert ant is modelled on *C. fortis*,** because it is the species the navigation and odour work is about (§2, §3), it is monodomous (one nest a colony, as the game has) and it is small (about 200 workers, 3 nests a hectare, nests about 40 m apart, 76–84 % of nest sites kept from year to year). *C. bicolor* (about 3,600 workers, polydomous, nests 9 m apart, sites kept 4.9 %) would need budding and several nests a colony (`ja-8fd`). **Design:** the forager-life and heat numbers are from *C. bicolor* and *C. bombycina*, so for *C. fortis* they are labelled borrowings; budding and the `Colony.queens[]` trait (`ja-8fd.1`) are not needed for a first build (one queen, as the existing colony has).

## 6. What is not known about *Cataglyphis* and fungi (the fungus game)

- No record of natural *Metarhizium* or *Beauveria* infection in any *Cataglyphis* species was found. *C. cursor* is a listed host of the ectoparasitic fungus *Aegeritella roussillonensis* (a host listing; **unverified**); a *C. mauritanica* study links colony genetic diversity to worker survival against a fungal entomopathogen (Springer s00265-023-03358-y; which fungus and the numbers unconfirmed). Other enemies in the records: the trematode *Dicrocoelium lanceatum* in *C. albicans*, eucharitid wasps in *C. bicolor* and *C. nodus* (AntWiki records; **unverified**).
- Social immunity, formic-acid or venom spraying, metapleural gland, corpse removal in *Cataglyphis*: **not found**; only general ant literature turned up, which is not evidence for this genus. Not searched separately: parasitic and slave-making ants of *Cataglyphis*.

**In the game:** the fungus's host range for this species is **design** ([fungus-policy §10](fungus-policy.md#10-more-colonies-and-other-species-planned-with-mr-8-ja-8fd)): no paper shows a desert-ant fungus. Pins to what the research does support: foragers are the exposed ones (outside, with higher immune investment), the colony is small, and there is no recorded undertaking, so the species ships **without corpse removal**, a visible difference from the generic colony and a lever for the fungus.

## The mechanic

The species is an **agent kind the one-policy shape must carry**. Cheapest slice first; every row has an "off" that leaves main bit for bit.

| Cue (sensed) | Sense row | Response | Local rule | Off | Retires or adds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Food odour gradient in the air | **new row** (§3) | steering of an empty-jawed forager | climb the gradient within range, else the existing search | no species, no gradient | adds one world field; no gap |
| Own body heat | own body | mode: return, rest | return at a heat threshold; rest in shade or nest to cool | no heat field | adds a body rule; no gap |
| Vector and route memory (no trails) | path integration, route memory | carrier steers by them | the existing vector, once route memory (`ja-lp4.16`) is built | trails on | route-memory moves from optional to a dependency |
| A finder's invitation | encounter rate | `leave` | a returning finder raises a nestmate's `returns` | no invitation | none |

**Situation and lanes.** The species' mode lane has *fewer* values than the generic ant's (`foodTrail` and `alarm` are constant "none"; a new `heat` field has two or three values). Because every situation field multiplies the question space, the lane is its own: its own legend, its own doctrine and its own precache. This is the case the descriptor must serve, and exactly the one that today's hard-coded `Situation` cannot: **one species' sense list is not another's.**

**What the species forces on the descriptor** (the point of picking it): the channel set per species (a species with `food`/`home`/`alarm` removed and no `raid`); per-species situation fields; a lane that does not exist for a species (no stance lane without raids); an ant whose action set has no `follow` mode; a per-species corpse behaviour (none); and per-species physiology (heat). Each is an acceptance criterion for `ja-fnd.5.3`'s test species, now with a real consumer instead of a made-up one.

**Assays.** Targets for `experiment.ts` (offline): (a) foragers do **not** form a trail (A3-style mill check passes trivially, and no channel is deposited); (b) trips are bursts at the cool end of the heat window and no forager survives a trip longer than its heat budget; (c) a forager whose vector fails finds home by search loops (existing assay); (d) a forager steered by food odour finds a patch faster than a blind search on the same seeds. Targets from the papers read: (b) forager loss about 16 % a day and a life of about 4–6 days (*C. bicolor*, **a borrowing**, summary only); (c)/(d) a forager turns in to food odour up to about 6 m and reaches nest odour within about 0.3 m (*C. fortis*; odour 2014 figures from a draft); a colony of about 200 workers with about 3 nests a hectare and nests about 40 m apart (*C. fortis*, full text); (e) an idle ant's exit chance rises past a speed threshold of about 8 cm/s and a recruiter's entry raises exits sevenfold in a minute, falling as density rises (*C. niger*, lab, full text). The heat-window figures are for *C. bombycina* and stay unset until the 1992 letter is read.

**Cost (estimated, from request size, not measured).** A new lane with a smaller legend and a four-field situation (`mode`, `tired`, `heat`, `enemy`; no trails): fewer states than the generic ant's, so fewer distinct questions. The tokens per question come from `npm run colony-cost` once the legend exists; the PRD estimate is below the generic ant's lane. No stance lane means no stance calls. A cached game spends nothing.

## Play

- **See:** a third colony on the map whose ants **never form lines**: single ants fan out at the start of the cool window, cross the open ground fast, and run for the nest or shade when the heat rises. The Learn drawer needs an entry (what you see: lone runners and no trails; what real ants do: *Cataglyphis* path integration and the thermal window; how the game models it).
- **Choose:** the fungus's levers. There is no trail for the fungus to bend, so its existing trail and alarm levers do **nothing** on this species; what reaches it is **foraging contact and heat**: the fungus can alter the heat a host feels (a host that thinks it is hotter turns back early), the odour of a host, and spread by the contact between an invitation's finder and its nestmate. These are **design**; no paper shows them. The owner chooses the fungus's levers for this host.
- **Counterplay and progression:** the generic colonies groom and bury their dead; this species has no recorded corpse removal, so it is an easier host but a smaller colony that gives less spread: a weaker reward per host but a faster takeover. Where it sits in the progression is an owner call.
- **Cost:** below the generic ant's lane (above); cached games spend nothing.
- **Risks:** a **cliff** (every forager leaves at the same moment, the outburst, so the colony's whole food income flips on and off by the hour: look for it in the intake curve); a **free lever** (a heat lever that makes the whole colony stay in is a cheap way to starve it: gate it by reach as the fungus's other levers are); **invisible effects** (the heat has to show on the field: ants stilting, shade seeking, a ground-temperature tint); **cost creep** (a new situation field must not re-ask the generic ant's cached answers: the species has its own lane and cache context).
- **Owner decisions needed:** see Open questions.
