# Steering: an ant weighs its guidance cues by their reliability and its own state, but trail following itself barely moves

*Research note for epic `ja-steer` (stage 1, research; PRD §S3). Written 2026-11-14, against code at `5fb6a90`. Read in full text (PubMed Central): Wystrach, Mangan & Webb 2015; Hoinville & Wehner 2018; Buehlmann, Mangan & Graham 2020 (first 100,000 characters); Czaczkes et al. 2011 and 2012; Czaczkes et al. 2015; Czaczkes & Heinze 2015; Dussutour et al. 2009; Wenig, Bach & Czaczkes 2021; Popp & Dornhaus 2024 (first 100,000 characters). Everything else comes from abstracts, database records or citing papers, and its numbers are marked **unverified**. Refused by this session's network policy: `journals.biologists.com`, `pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`, `link.springer.com` (login redirect), `www.biorxiv.org`, `www.sussex.ac.uk`, `epub.uni-regensburg.de`, `epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de`, `dipot.ulb.ac.be`, `web.stanford.edu`, `www.math.utah.edu`, `groups.csail.mit.edu`. It builds on [route-memory](route-memory.md), [forager-trips](forager-trips.md), [nest-search](nest-search.md), [trails](trails.md), [combat](combat.md), [desert-ant](desert-ant.md) and [ant-communication](ant-communication.md), and does not repeat them.*

The game today: for an ant that carries nothing, the colony policy picks one of four modes (`explore`, `follow`, `return`, `fight`) per situation, and each mode is a fixed steering routine in `web/src/colony/steer.ts`. Inside a routine the cues have constant weights (random turning is `noise × wander` while exploring and a quarter of that while following or homing; the home trail leads a homing ant by the share `homeTrail`). The proposal under study is to let the policy set a weight per cue per situation instead. The question this note answers: does a real forager combine trail, home vector, last find, alarm, nestmates and random search into one heading, and does it weight them differently by situation and by its own state (experience, success, load, uncertainty)?

## 1. A homing ant averages its vector and its view, and the vector's weight grows with its length

- *Cataglyphis velox* trained on a 7 m route were caught after 0, 1, 3 or 7 m of their homeward run and released at a familiar spot 1.5 m from the nest, where the vector and the view pointed 110° apart. The longer the vector, the closer the heading to the vector: the 0 m group went nestward, the 1 and 3 m groups took intermediate headings, the 7 m group followed the vector. Heading scatter also fell as the vector grew. The best of four models weights each cue by its expected directional variance (AIC −6.04, against −2.76 for a distance-with-threshold rule), with the view as certain as a 0.95 m vector. Source: Wystrach, Mangan & Webb 2015, *Proc R Soc B* 282:20151484, full text. The species is *C. velox* in Spain, not *Melophorus* as [route-memory §2](route-memory.md#2-how-the-two-combine-the-vectors-weight-falls-as-it-fails) and [forager-trips §2](forager-trips.md#2-the-ant-reads-its-uncertainty-from-its-vectors-length-not-its-path) say.
- **Against true uncertainty:** ants that walked about 10 m in a pot without lengthening a 3 m vector headed exactly like plain 3 m ants (WW test p = 0.85), though their real uncertainty was larger. The authors conclude the ant uses the vector's length as a proxy, a fixed and probably innate curve, not a learnt estimate (same paper).
- *Melophorus bagoti* also average celestial and terrestrial directions, weighted by reliability (Legge, Wystrach, Spetch & Cheng 2014, *J Exp Biol* 217:4159; abstract seen only in part, **unverified**). As refitted by Hoinville & Wehner 2018: ants trained on an 11 m route, tested in three conditions (labelled 4, 32 and 64 m) with conflicts of about 132°, 117° and 65°; the 64 m group was not oriented (Rayleigh p = 0.74). What the three distances were could not be checked without the paper.
- A model that sums one vector per guidance system, each as long as that system is certain, reproduces those two experiments, a displaced-beacon test in *C. fortis* and clock-shifted honeybees (Hoinville & Wehner 2018, *PNAS* 115:2824, full text). It measures nothing new; parameters are fitted, the 7 m condition misfits (p < 0.001), and in large conflicts the sum swings to the more reliable cue, so it behaves like a switch there. The authors call calibrating the certainties against each other "an open question".
- Weights also shift with the place and the ant's history: ants from cluttered habitats run off less of their vector when displaced than ants from open ground; experienced ants with a bigger mismatch between stored and seen views run off a shorter part of it; and an ant re-running a familiar route that never reaches the nest eventually turns against its view, sooner when the opposing vector is longer and in an open panorama (Buehlmann, Mangan & Graham 2020, *Anim Cogn* 23:1129, review read in full, citing Buehlmann 2011, Cheng 2012, Schwarz 2017; Wystrach, Schwarz, Graham & Cheng 2019, *Anim Cogn* 22:213, abstract, **unverified**).

**In the game:** rows "Path integration" and "Route memory (design)". The biology supports a weight on the vector that rises with the vector's length, and one on route memory that rises with familiarity; both are continuous functions of what the ant senses, not per-situation choices. A policy-set weight would be a lookup of that curve by a coarse vector-length bucket. Letting the policy invent the curve per situation is **design**; route-memory's switch (vector fails → route) stays a valid simplification of the large-conflict case.

## 2. Which cue leads switches with the trip's phase: food odour outbound, vector inbound

- *C. fortis* going out follow a food odour plume over their vector to a feeder; homing *C. fortis* ignore their nest's CO₂ plume until their vector says they are near home (Buehlmann et al. 2012, 2013; already read in [desert-ant §3](desert-ant.md#3-they-find-food-by-smell-not-by-following-one-another) and [nest-search](nest-search.md#near-the-nest-an-ant-whose-vector-has-run-out-follows-the-nests-own-smell)). The 2020 review sums it up: "PI overrides olfactory information and ants only respond to nest odours when they are close to home", while for food "olfactory information overrides PI" (Buehlmann, Mangan & Graham 2020, full text).
- In wood ants (*Formica rufa* group) odour and vision add up: "in some situations cues act additively" (Buehlmann, Aussel & Graham 2020, as cited in the review; **unverified**).

**In the game:** rows "Path integration", "Landmarks and entrance odour" and "Food odour in the air (desert ant)". This is the clearest case of a cue's weight set by the ant's state: load (carrying or not) and the vector's remaining length gate which cue leads. The game already gates nest smell on a run-off vector and the desert ant's plume on the outward trip. A policy that re-weights by phase would be choosing what biology already fixes; the measured rule is a gate, not a free weight.

## 3. Private memory beats the trail after one visit in *Lasius niger*, but the trail still steadies it

- *L. niger* foragers that met a trail on the other branch from where they had fed went with memory: 82–100 % chose their remembered branch, more after 3 visits than after 1, and no less when the trail had been laid by 20 ants rather than 1. Naive ants followed a trail on only 61.6 % (1 ant) and 70.2 % (20 ants) of choices. A competing trail did not slow the choice. Source: Grüter, Czaczkes & Ratnieks 2011, *Behav Ecol Sociobiol* 65:141, abstract. **Unverified.**
- *L. niger* rapidly switch to their own memory of visual cues, independent of trail concentration; Argentine ants (*Linepithema humile*) stay on the trail longer, and nearly all lay trail at first against half of *L. niger* (Aron, Beckers, Deneubourg & Pasteels 1993, *Insectes Soc* 40:369, abstract). Memory and motivation change response thresholds and error rates differently in *L. niger*, *L. humile* and *Euprenolepis procera* (von Thienen, Metzler & Witte 2016, *Behav Ecol Sociobiol* 70:393, abstract). Ants trained to a poor 0.25 M feeder kept to memory (60–75 %) until the new trail also carried a droplet of 1.5 M sugar, when 75 % took it (Czaczkes & Beckwith, bioRxiv 10.1101/219980, preprint, abstract). All **unverified**.
- **The trail still counts for an experienced ant.** On a 21 cm walkway, *L. niger* with route memory walked 30 % faster and 30 % straighter on trail than off it; stepping off a trail raised U-turns with or without memory; and experienced ants that stepped off cut their own laying by 59 %. Trail strength (1 or 20 passages) changed none of it: the effect is all or nothing (Czaczkes, Grüter, Jones & Ratnieks 2011, *Biol Lett* 7:521, full text). On alternating routes (32 % errors against 3 % on repeating ones) a trail cut errors by 30 % (Czaczkes et al. 2013, *J Exp Biol* 216:188, abstract, **unverified**); a later test found no evidence that pheromone helps route learning (Czaczkes, Weichselgartner, Bernadou & Heinze 2016, *PLoS ONE*, abstract, **unverified**).

**In the game:** rows "Trail pheromones" and "Path integration" (the last find, [forager-trips §3](forager-trips.md#3-foragers-go-back-to-where-they-last-found-food), built and off). The weight of trail against memory is set by **species** and by **experience** (one rewarded visit flips it), not by trail strength. A per-ant "visits to this find" count, which the body already holds as the last-find vector, is the sensed state; the policy choosing the weight per situation is **design**. A trail also lowers wander noise for an ant that already knows the way (§5).

## 4. Uncertainty shows in what an ant lays more clearly than in where it walks

- *L. niger* in a T-maze whose feeder moved after 1–15 training visits (413 ants): returning ants that had just met the change, or had just erred, laid more trail (Z = 7.61 and 6.40, p < 0.001); outgoing ants about to err were less likely to lay (Z = −6.19); laying fell with experience. The authors suggest ants "can measure the reliability of their own memories" but do not claim metacognition (Czaczkes & Heinze 2015, *Proc R Soc B* 282:20150679, full text).
- Persistence at a feeder that stopped paying rose with training and levelled at about 10 visits, with or without pheromone; a model using memory and pheromone together gathered more than either alone, and assumed memory overrides pheromone (Czaczkes, Czaczkes, Iglhaut & Heinze 2015, *Proc R Soc B* 282:20142723, full text).

**In the game:** row "Own body" (experience, a recent miss) feeding the trail the ant lays, not the heading. It belongs to the laying response, not to a steering weight; listed so the steering design does not claim it.

## 5. Following a trail is close to fixed: neither task, experience, distance nor punishment moves it much

- *L. niger* scouts, recruits and shuttlers chose the trail branch 83 %, 82 % and 74 % of the time, not significantly different, with large variation between trials (Czaczkes, Castorena, Schürch & Heinze 2017, *Physiol Entomol* 42:91, abstract, **unverified**).
- About 20 % of *L. niger* foragers ignore a trail, but which ones is not repeatable an hour later: no specialist non-followers (Koch & Czaczkes 2020/21, *Ecol Entomol*, doi:10.1111/een.12995, abstract, **unverified**).
- Trail following "is not modulated by previous visit to food location, distance travelled, or travel direction" in *L. niger*; distance walked before the trail did change straightness, speed and distance walked afterwards (Poissonnier, Winter, Olivera-Rodriguez, Werneke & Czaczkes 2026, *Insectes Soc*, abstract, **unverified**).
- *L. niger* that met quinine (and in one test a shock) at the end of a trail learnt within about 10 trials to ignore it (a side bias), but never to avoid it, staying at chance through 25 trials; they learnt to avoid a lemon odour at once (Wenig, Bach & Czaczkes 2021, *J Exp Biol* 224:jeb242454, full text).
- The dose-response of trail choice differs in shape between species and follows Weber's law (von Thienen, Metzler, Choe & Witte 2014, *Behav Ecol Sociobiol* 68:1611, abstract, **unverified**).
- **Against:** *L. niger* scouts were less attracted to colony odour and followed trails less than recruits, which had lower trail thresholds (Detrain, Pereira & Fourcassié 2019, *Behav Ecol Sociobiol* 73, abstract, **unverified**); §3's memory override is the other exception.

**In the game:** row "Trail pheromones". This is the strongest evidence against free re-weighting: given that an ant takes the trail at all, how faithfully it follows is a species constant near 75–85 %. What moves is whether memory overrides it (§3) and how the ant walks once on or off it. A policy weight on the trail that varies widely by situation has no support; a per-species constant has.

## 6. Some error in following helps the colony, and the best amount depends on how fast food changes

- Deneubourg, Pasteels & Verhaeghe 1983 (*J Theor Biol* 105:259) modelled recruits that miss the trail as finders of new food, and argued that less accurate recruitment pays when food sources are many. Recruitment accuracy differs by species: about 75 % of *Tapinoma erraticum* recruits reach the food at the start against about 20 % in *Tetramorium impurum* (Deneubourg, Aron, Goss & Pasteels, as cited by a citing paper). Abstract-level and secondary; **unverified**.
- *Pheidole megacephala* colonies switched branches when the short one was blocked and reopened (18 of 21, then 16 of 21 back by the last 30 min), far faster than the deterministic model allows (over 250 min); adding noise reproduced it, and more noise switched faster. The noise is a fitted model term, not a measured per-ant error, and *L. niger*'s lower noise would not switch within the experiment (Dussutour, Beekman, Nicolis & Meyer 2009, *Proc R Soc B* 276:4353, full text).
- A model of *Lasius japonicus* finds the best spread of individual noise across a colony shifts with the feeding environment (Shiraishi et al. 2019, *J Theor Biol* 465:7, abstract, **unverified**).

**In the game:** row "Own body" (no external cue): the random-search weight (`wander`). The biology argues for a noise level per species, tuned to how changeable its food is, and against zero noise. Noise chosen per situation by the policy is **design**; no paper measured an ant changing its error rate with its situation.

## 7. Search turns tight after a find and wide when lost

- *Formica schaufussi* foragers' paths split into straight travel and local search with a high turning rate and lower speed; search after carbohydrate lasts longer and stays nearer the find than after protein. The authors say short-term experience does not set the pattern: it is a predisposition tied to the food type, not to age (Traniello, Kozol & Fournier 1992, *Psyche* 99:87; Fourcassié & Traniello 1993, *J Insect Behav* 6:287; 1994, *Anim Behav* 48:69; abstracts, **unverified**).
- *Melophorus bagoti* going out to a removed feeder search round its place, a Brownian-like walk whose size depends on the food type; nest searches scale with path-integration error instead (Schultheiss & Cheng 2013, *Behav Ecol* 24:128, abstract and thesis summary, **unverified**). Nest search widening with trip length is in [nest-search](nest-search.md#the-search-is-wider-after-a-longer-trip); return to the last find is in [forager-trips §3](forager-trips.md#3-foragers-go-back-to-where-they-last-found-food).
- Distance walked before meeting a trail changes later straightness and speed in *L. niger* (Poissonnier et al. 2026, §5).

**In the game:** rows "Path integration" (the last find), "Taste" (the food type) and "Own body". Turning rate is the one weight the biology clearly lets the situation set: low while travelling, high at the spot of a find or where the vector ran out, and its extent set by food type or vector length. That fits a policy-set "search tightness" per situation; its numbers are **design**.

## 8. No forager is shown steering by nearby nestmates off the trail

- In five *Temnothorax rugatulus* colonies tracked for 5 h in a 2 × 3 m arena, ants did not turn toward or away from denser footprints (p = 0.68); they walked about 12 % straighter and 11 % faster on 0 than on 20 estimated footprints, much of it explained by individual and place differences. Only 34 encounters happened more than 10 cm from the nest (Popp & Dornhaus 2024, *PLoS ONE* 19:e0299432, full text; correlational).
- Argentine ant explorers walk more convoluted paths at high density and straighter ones at low density, but path shape did not change within 40 min as density rose (Gordon 1995, *Anim Behav* 50:995, abstract, **unverified**). No mechanism (encounter, footprint) is given.
- Spacing without interaction: *Cataglyphis* keep to the sector of their last success (Buchkremer & Reinhold 2008, *Behav Ecol* 19:984) and harvester foragers to a direction (Fewell 1990, as cited), which spreads foragers with no rule about one another. Adler & Gordon 2003 (*Am Nat* 162:529) model ranges between *Pogonomyrmex barbatus* colonies, not spacing within one. Abstracts, **unverified**.
- *L. niger* recruits are more attracted to colony odour than scouts (Detrain et al. 2019, §5); attraction to nestmates themselves while searching was not found.

**In the game:** row "Encounter rate (antennation)". A weight on nearby nestmates (attract or repel) has no direct basis: **design**. The only lead is density-dependent turning (more turning when crowded), which an encounter rate could drive.

## 9. Alarm: dose decides the sign; what the ant is doing decides who approaches

- Faint alarm draws, strong alarm repels or enrages, and a meeting with an enemy raises alarm (combat, [Graded alarm](combat.md#graded-alarm-a-little-draws-a-lot-repels-or-enrages)); defence is an individual threshold raised by alarm and encounters ([stance](stance.md)). Nothing new was researched here.

**In the game:** row "Alarm". Its weight already depends on dose and on the ant's stance; a policy weight per situation would repeat that. How a forager trades alarm against its trail or vector was not found (below).

## 10. Scouts and recruits differ, but as states more than as kinds of ant

- Scouts, patrollers and recruits differ in trail and colony-odour responses (Detrain et al. 2019, §5), yet trail ignoring is not repeatable (Koch & Czaczkes 2020/21) and task state did not change following accuracy (Czaczkes et al. 2017). Colonies differ consistently in exploratory activity, the best predictor of their foraging success, and ants that lay more trail learn routes worse (Pasquier & Grüter 2016, *Behav Ecol* 27:1702, abstract, **unverified**).

**In the game:** row "Own body". Differences between ants are better modelled as states an ant passes through (naive, recruited, experienced) than as fixed personalities; a colony-level exploration level has support. Per-ant fixed weights are **design**.

## What was not found

- Any measurement of an individual forager combining more than two cues at once (trail, vector, last find and alarm together).
- How a forager weighs alarm against its trail or its vector, or alarm on a foraging trail changing trail following.
- Load (carrying vs not) changing trail-following fidelity; hunger or colony starvation changing it (reported only for trail laying and recruitment).
- Any ant changing its own random-turning (error) rate by situation; noise is measured only as a colony-level model term.
- Attraction to or avoidance of nestmates themselves (not their trails or footprints) by a searching forager.
