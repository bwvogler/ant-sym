# The fungus's unlocks: a small landing patch, mats and new patches, two budgets, a lichen partner

*Research + design note for epic `ja-vlla` (PRD §85). Written 2026-11-22, against code at `0d2738f8`. Owner's ask (2026-11-22): additions to the fungus that are unlocked rather than bought from one pool: ant-specific abilities from mass in ants, free-fungus abilities from mass outside; a downstream algae or plant partner (a lichen: use rock, break it down, spread across it) unlocked by contact; a new patch from 7 completely connected tiles; and a smaller first piece. Searches ran on 2026-11-22 through Europe PMC and the PMC full-text service; Wiley, Taylor & Francis and ScienceDirect pages were bot-walled, so Boddy 1999, Trinci 1974, Dowson et al. 1988, Chen et al. 2000 and Armstrong's rates were not read: where the text leans on them it says "(from memory; not fetched)". This is ecology and mycelial architecture only.*

**The question.** When a mycelium meets a rich place, does it settle there, and what do the lichen-forming fungi do with rock and a photosynthetic partner, that a game fungus could be given the same way?

## TL;DR

- **Dense mats where it is rich: only partly supported.** The slow, dense "phalanx" against fast, sparse "guerrilla" contrast is real, but as a difference between *species*; no paper read measures the same species branching more on rich than on poor substrate, and one sugar fungus (*Mortierella*) is dense in a rich habitat (§1). A thickening rule is mostly **design**.
- **A new focus at a new resource: supported.** A cord-forming mycelium that reaches a new resource aggregates the cords that join old and new and grows out from the new base (§2). A new patch at a resource has a source; a patch at 7 filled tiles has none, and the number 7 is **design**. Hyphae reach a resource only where thickening is allowed (at a colonised corpse, owner 2026-11-22), so flowers form near resources because thicken does; the conversion rule itself is any flower.
- **Lichens break rock with acid and hyphae, slowly, from the edge in:** oxalic acid and hyphae penetrating the grain, and wet-dry swelling (§3). The photobiont feeds the fungus carbon; contact turns the symbiosis genes on (§4).
- **Fungi that are not lichens can touch algae too** (yeast and *Chlamydomonas* in the lab, *Mortierella* taking in *Nannochloropsis*): an alga-fungus partnership does not need a lichen (§4). *Metarhizium* with algae: not found, so the partner is **design**.
- **The two budgets and the 7-tile landing patch are design:** no source gives them. The repo's notes already separate the fungus's soil life from its life in hosts (`docs/fungus-body.md` §1, §3).
- **What the game does today:** one mass pool and one currency (mutations, derived from growth in the soil and in hosts); a patch is a counter plus a disc of 19 cells; rock is impassable; there is nothing to touch (no plants, algae or lichen). §5.

## 1. Dense where it is rich: a species axis, not a measured response

- **Phalanx and guerrilla.** Outgrowth patterns of cord-formers run from "slowly extending search fronts densely populated by hyphae, which are unlikely to miss new resources" (*Hypholoma fasciculare*) to "more rapidly extending but sparser systems" (*Phanerochaete velutina*) and, at the far end, *Armillaria*. The slow-dense ones are "phalanx", short-range; the fast-sparse ones "guerrilla", long-range; plants have the same split (clump against runner) (Fricker, Heaton, Jones & Boddy 2017, *Microbiol Spectr* 5:FUNK-0033-2017, full text). No numbers are given.
- **A gradient of connectivity, and a counter-example.** *Mucor*, in nutrient-rich habitats, makes a sparse, tree-like, fast network of very low hyphal density; *Phanerochaete* and *Mortierella* sit near the dense, many-loops end, though *Mortierella* is also a sugar fungus of rich habitats, which cuts against "rich means sparse" (Aguilar-Trigueros, Boddy, Rillig & Fricker 2022, *ISME Commun* 2:2, full text).
- **Little plasticity within a species.** In 30 clones each of cord-formers with soil grazers, shape changes were limited and species-specific, not the extreme plasticity usually assumed for modular organisms; fungi can still raise local occupancy "by increasing branching rates" in patchy resources, citing Boddy 1999 (Aguilar-Trigueros, Boddy & Fricker 2025, *Ecol Lett* 28:e70281, full text; the claim about branching rates is second-hand here).
- **Branching frequency.** That branching is more frequent, and the hyphal growth unit smaller, on richer medium is Trinci 1974 and Boddy 1999 (from memory; not fetched).
- **A solid mat on a resource:** the nearest thing read is a zoned ring of mycelium in leaf litter (*Clitocybe nebularis*, Fricker 2017). No paper read describes a solid mat.

**In the game:** a tip thickening to fill its neighbours when well supplied has partial support (species-level dense foragers, and Boddy 1999 as recalled) and no measurement behind it: it is **design**, stated as such. The cue that says "rich" should be a place the game already calls a resource: a colonised corpse (`docs/hyphal-growth.md` §6: a hypha that reaches a corpse grows over it). The thickening is a growth-lane answer over what the tip senses; the local rule, which plays when there is no answer, never thickens, so off is today's game.

## 2. A new focus at a new resource

- **Cords re-form round a new resource.** When a mycelium meets a new resource, the parts that connect old and new aggregate into cords and non-connecting mycelium dies back; short-range foragers reallocate biomass "rapidly and extensively", leaving "often only the connective cord and outward growth from the newly colonized resource after a few months"; long-range ones reallocate little unless the new resource is considerably bigger (Fricker et al. 2017, full text).
- **Moving house depends on distance and size.** *P. velutina* inoculum blocks on unsterilised soil moved to a bait more often when it was near (1 cm) than far (15 cm), and to large baits more than small; bait weight loss (energy gained) and hyphal coverage on soil (foraging cost) were the drivers (Fukasawa & Ishii 2023, *Front Cell Dev Biol* 11:1244673, full text; percentages not extracted).
- **How big a new base is:** only bait volumes (4 and 8 cm³) and 20×20×10 mm blocks in these papers; no threshold size at which a focus forms. Dowson, Rayner & Boddy 1988 (*FEMS Microbiol Lett* 53:291) is the classic on foraging between discontinuous resources (not read).

- **Translocation makes a new base a source.** Translocation lets a mycelium colonise low-resource substrate, the colonising mycelium turning from a sink into a source of resource (Lindahl & Olsson 2004, *Mycologist* 18:79, abstract only).

**In the game:** a **new patch is a new focus at a resource**, which is where the biology puts it: where hyphae have filled a hex and its six neighbours (7 cells, **design**: no paper gives a size) the 7 become a patch, a new source of mass and spores. Flowers form near resources only because the thicken cue is a colonised corpse (owner, 2026-11-22: §1); the conversion rule is any flower that contains no cell of an existing patch (the landing patch's own cells never count, or the first patch would become a second at once). **The new patch's mass comes from the hyphae** (owner, 2026-11-22, on the review of #299): the seven cells' mass (7 × 0.25) is already in the hyphae, so the patch adds capacity and a spore source but no mass. Today every patch, the landing and a queen's founding included, adds a start of 4 mass (`Evolution.root`: `patches++`, `mass += BODY.start`); a flower therefore takes its own entry point that skips the start, so today's patches stay bit-exact.

## 3. Lichens and rock

- **Acid and hyphae.** Fungi secrete organic acids, "especially oxalic acid", which acidify the surface and dissolve minerals; hyphae penetrate the substrate; calcium and magnesium oxalates and carbonates re-form round the hyphae and can fill cracks, so the effect on stone is mixed, and lichen biomineralisation can even consolidate it (Paiva et al. 2026, *J Fungi* 12:128, full text; a review of stone heritage, not lichen-specific). Fungi, lichens included, are weathering agents by biomechanical and biochemical means (Burford, Fomina & Gadd 2003, *Mineral Mag* 67:1127, abstract only).
- **A mechanism in grit.** In the Atacama, lichens grew attached to grit stones with single hyphae penetrating them; algal partners reached the middle of grits; fog wetting and drying swelled organisms and mineral, and pH shifts broke the grit down into a loose soil (Jung et al. 2020, *iScience* 23:101647, full text). No mm a year is given.
- **Rates.** A review of lichen weathering (Chen, Blume & Beyer 2000, *Catena* 39:121) was not read, so there are no weathering rates here.
- **Spread.** Crustose and foliose lichens grow as near-circular disks; small ones slowly, with a rising radial rate; large ones at a roughly constant radial speed, the edge doing the work (a model predicts a maximum of about 26 mm a year against the fastest reported, about 13 mm a year, *Parmelia*) (Seminara, Fritz, Brenner & Pringle 2018, *J R Soc Interface* 15:20180063, full text read by the search; the 26 mm and 13 mm figures were not re-verified by a second reader, and the 13 mm is **unverified**, second-hand from this paper's references). Typical crustose rates well under 1 mm a year are from memory; not fetched.

**In the game:** growing across and into rock, and breaking it down, is a late unlock (M4) with a slow, edge-led spread; it gives the fungus rock as ground it can enter, as the *borer* gave it undug soil. The speed is **design** timing (real lichens are far slower than a game minute).

## 4. The partner, and contact

- **What the alga gives, and what it wants.** In co-culture of the lichen fungus and alga of *Usnea hakonensis*, the algae supply photosynthetic product and the fungus supplies phosphorus and nitrogen compounds; the fungal cell wall is modified at fungal-algal contact sites and a hydrophobic layer ensheathes both, and the symbiosis genes switch on at contact (Kono et al. 2020, *BMC Genomics* 21:671, full text). Lichens are active only when wet and lit (Palmqvist 2000: from memory; not fetched).
- **Fungi that are not lichens do it too.** Yeast and *Chlamydomonas reinhardtii* formed an obligate mutualism on reciprocal carbon and nitrogen exchange; *Aspergillus nidulans* hyphae made tight wall-to-wall contacts with the alga like a lichen interface, with no hyphal penetration of algal cells (Hom & Murray 2014, *Science* 345:94, full text). *Nannochloropsis oceanica* cells were taken into *Mortierella elongata* hyphae after close contact, with carbon and nitrogen moving between them (Du et al. 2019, *eLife* 8:e47815, full text).
- **A plant partner.** The repo already has *Metarhizium* as a root endophyte moving insect nitrogen to plants (Behie et al. 2012, *Science* 336:1576, `docs/fungus-body.md` §1).
- **Not found:** *Metarhizium* with algae.

**In the game:** the partner is a downstream organism the fungus meets: **contact** with an alga or a plant unlocks it, and with it its senses and abilities (rock, spreading across it). The partner, what there is on the map to touch, and its own senses are for the owner (open, M4); this note only records that the biology supports contact as the trigger and carbon for nutrients as the trade, and that a *Metarhizium* partnership is **design**.

## 5. What the game does today

- **One pool.** `Evolution.mass` is the mycelium's mass; `growth` adds new fungus made on the ground (`grow`), from corpses (`feed`) and in hosts (`replicate`); mutations are `0.25 × growth − spent`, and every level costs one rising price (PRD §80). Mass in ants exists only as a display (`inHosts`, `ui/stats.ts`): the sum of fungus load over living ants of the strain, not part of `Evolution.mass`. Nothing is priced from either.
- **A patch** is a count (`Evolution.patches`) plus a disc of cells, and each one adds a start of 4 mass from nothing (`Evolution.root`): `Fungus.root` takes every non-rock cell within `LANDING.radius` (1: 7 cells, a hex and its six neighbours, since §85 M1; it was 2, 19 cells), makes them `base` and hypha `sources`, and sends three tips out of the rim. Capacity is `capacity × patches`. Patches are made at the landing and by a founding queen carrying the fungus.
- **Rock** is impassable to hyphae (`enterable`, `hyphae.ts`); the *borer* only lets them into undug soil. **Nothing on the map is a plant, alga or lichen.**
- **Hyphae** are one cell wide and turn away from their own hypha, so a cell with all six neighbours hyphal is rare: measured on seeds 1–6, ten minutes, shipped answers, the number of cells with all six neighbours hyphal was 4–6 in every game and did not grow between minutes 3 and 10, so they are the landing disc's own interior: the hyphae outside it never added one.

## The mechanic

Four milestones, each its own PR, the first the smallest (owner: "the first patch should be smaller"):

1. **M1: the landing patch is the 7-cell flower** (`ja-vlla.2` the assay, `ja-vlla.3` the build). `LANDING.radius` 2 becomes 1: a hex and its six neighbours, for the landing and for a queen's founding. Built (PR #312): the parameter is `LANDING.radius`, and `progression.mjs --landing 2` gives the old landing back. It shrinks the spore ground (7 cells hold at most `7 × maxSpores`, against 19) and the first patch's capacity use, so P1 is re-tuned by the worst seed; no Jev change. The assay (`ja-vlla.2`) measured the whole change, spores included, and P1 holds (PRD §85).
2. **M2: mats and new patches** (`ja-vlla.4`–`.6`; owner: "hyphae must fill them"). A growth-lane answer, **thicken**: the tip fills the open, enterable neighbours of its cell, HYPHA_MASS each from its supply. A hypha cell a tip has just thickened round, whose six neighbours are all hyphae, is a flower (built, PR for `ja-vlla.5`; a dense web that never thickened is not looked at: `ja-vlla.9`), and it becomes a patch only if none of its seven cells is in an existing patch (not in `base`, not in `sources`): layer 0 only (as every root today), the mass from the hyphae (an entry point that skips the 4 a landing adds), three tips out of the rim. The answer changes the lane's legend (`GROWTH_BRAIN_VERSION` 5): a re-ask of about $0.002–0.004 an aim today, more with the fifth field, and a free `--import`. The cue for "a resource is here" is a colonised corpse beside the tip (owner, 2026-11-22): a fifth situation field, so 48 situations become 96.
3. **M3: two budgets** (`ja-vlla.7`; design with the owner first). Ant abilities (infectivity, persistence, evasion and the reach upgrades) from fungus made in hosts; free-fungus abilities (mycelium, knots, fruiting, stalks) from mass outside. Open: stock or earnings; one price or two; which is which.
4. **M4: the lichen partner** (`ja-vlla.8`; design with the owner first): something to touch, the partner's own senses and abilities, using rock.

For every milestone, off is today bit for bit and the goldens stay exact until a milestone re-records them.

## Play

- **See:** M1: a smaller purple flower at the landing instead of a disc. M2: where hyphae reach something rich, they thicken into a fan of cells, and when they close a flower it turns into a new patch (a new base with its own spores) the player can watch spread from. M3: two meters in the drawer, one for ants and one for the ground. M4: algae or plants on the map and, once touched, a partner that creeps across rock.
- **Choose:** M1 none (the start is smaller). M2: the aim steers where it thickens ("settle where there is food" against "keep reaching"): the first new say in where the fungus plants a second base. M3: which budget to grow, so which abilities. M4: whether to seek the partner.
- **Progression:** a smaller start is a slower start: P1 is the guard. A new patch takes its mass from the hyphae (owner), so a second patch costs the reach it did not grow; capacity is the reward.
- **Cost:** none in M1. M2 re-asks the growth lane (about $0.002–0.004 an aim today, PRD §54.4, once; cached games spend nothing; more with a fifth field), and a fifth situation field would raise the lane's tokens a question (to be measured with `scripts/colony-cost.mjs`). M3 and M4 are not asked of Jev unless they add a lane.
- **Risks:** a **cliff** early (M1 shrinks spores and capacity at the start: worst seed of P1); a **runaway** (capacity grows with patches, so a patch that is cheap to make would snowball: it takes its seven cells' mass from the hyphae and only forms where a flower of hyphae stands outside every patch, and the rate is the number to watch); an **invisible** rule (a patch forming with no sign: it needs a mark and a notice); a **free lever** (the partner skipping the slope: it is unlocked by contact and starts weak); a mat rule with only species-level support, which a reviewer can fairly call design (it is marked so).

## 6. M3: the two budgets, options (2026-11-23, for the owner)

**What the literature gives, and what it does not.** *For a fungus with two lives:* *Metarhizium* is rhizosphere-competent (Hu & St. Leger 2002) and persists in soil for months to a year or more (field summaries, unverified: `fungus-body.md` §1); as a root endophyte it passes insect nitrogen to plants (28 % and 32 % of a bean's and a switchgrass's nitrogen in microcosms, Behie et al. 2012) and receives plant carbon, more when an insect is present (Behie et al. 2017, *Nat Commun* 8:14245, from search summaries only, not read in full). *For ant-facing traits coming from ants:* lines passed through grooming ant groups for ten cycles evolved more spores and less of the cue groomers detect (*Nat Ecol Evol* 2023, press summary only). *For a rising price:* a hundredfold more mutation supply bought far less than a hundredfold faster adaptation (de Visser et al. 1999, bacterial). *Not found:* any source that ties an upgrade to where its growth was made (soil growth buying soil traits), or a trade-off between soil competence and virulence (the queries on in-host growth and virulence were refused by the search tool's filter, so they were **not read**), or an unlock that fires when a stock passes a level. The two budgets are design, as the TL;DR says; the repo's licence stays that a mutation can arise in any replication (`fungus-body.md` §3).

**What the game does today.** One pool: mutations = `MUTATION` (0.25) × growth, where growth is credited by `Evolution.grow` (the mycelium on the ground), `feed` (a colonised corpse) and `replicate` (load passed on by contact in hosts); one price, `round(1.5^kept)` over every level kept. The fourteen upgrades fall into two groups: **ant-facing**, 18 levels (infectivity 3, persistence 2, evasion 2, stealth 2, senses 1, recognition 2, trails 2, royal 2, odour weapon 1, crown 1), and **the fungus's own**, 8 levels (mycelium 2, knots 2, stalks 2, fruiting 2).

**Measured** (`progression.mjs --evolve --hyphae --growth --flow --split --seconds 600`, seeds 1–8, on main; where the growth that mutations come from was made):

| seed | ground | corpses | hosts | outside, of all |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 385 | 5 | 11,537 | 3.3 % |
| 2 | 691 | 26 | 23,653 | 2.9 % |
| 3 | 421 | 21 | 20,150 | 2.1 % |
| 4 | 403 | 26 | 25,500 | 1.7 % |
| 5 | 2,091 | 94 | 31,004 | 6.6 % |
| 6 | 942 | 82 | 20,327 | 4.8 % |
| 7 | 410 | 0 | 13,155 | 3.0 % |
| 8 | 398 | 70 | 22,323 | 2.1 % |

So **93 to 98 % of today's mutations come from load passed on in hosts** (a rate of passing, not a stock of fungus in ants), and 2–7 % from the ground. The mycelium's growth stops at what its patches hold, which is why it is small. Priced with its own kept count, the fungus's own 8 levels cost 1 + 2 + 2 + 3 + 5 + 8 + 11 + 17 = **49 mutations**, and the ground's 10-minute earnings are 98 to 546 mutations (0.25 × 390 to 2,185): every seed could buy all eight in ten minutes, and the ant-facing 18 levels cost 2,954, which the hosts' 2,884 to 7,751 mutations reach on seven of eight seeds. With **one shared price** the fungus's own levels would cost `round(1.5^kept)` over all kept levels, which is thousands once the ant levels are bought: unaffordable from 98 mutations.

**Options.**
- **A. Two pools, each spent like mutations today, each with its own rising price (recommended).** Ant-facing levels cost the ants pool (0.25 × growth in hosts); the fungus's own levels cost the free pool (0.25 × growth on the ground and from corpses). Each price counts only its own kept levels, so each tree is sized to its pool (49 against 98–546; 2,954 against 2,884–7,751). *For:* it is the owner's wording (budgets, spent); it reuses `Evolution`'s spend, keep and price; each budget buys what its growth site suggests. *Against:* the free pool is a few percent of the whole and nearly all of it is the landing's logistic growth, so the *Mycelium* upgrade (capacity) is also what feeds it, a flywheel (seed 5, which grows seven patches, earns four times seed 1); two meters in the drawer; the early game moves (ant levels come from their own cheaper price, the fungus's own from a small pool), so P1 must be re-assayed.
- **B. Stocks: unlock a level when the stock passes a threshold** (fungus in ants; mycelium mass outside, 42 to 279 at ten minutes). *For:* visible, no spending, no pool to run dry. *Against:* no choice (everything unlocks in time), which drops the player's say in what to grow; the owner's own word was "budgets"; no source gives thresholds; the "fungus in ants" stock is not what the game counts (it counts load passed on).
- **C. One pool as today, but split the price by group** (the ant levels and the fungus's own levels each priced by their own kept count, one currency). *For:* the smallest change; no second meter. *Against:* it does not separate the budgets, so the owner's ask is not met; the ants pool would still buy the fungus's own levels cheaply.

**Decided (owner, 2026-11-23, shown the options above):** **A**, two pools each with its own rising price; *fruiting* and *stalks* are the free pool's (the fungus's own 8 levels: mycelium, knots, stalks, fruiting); a colonised corpse's substance (`feed`) feeds the free pool with ground growth. Ant-facing levels (18) are paid from growth in hosts. The asked questions follow, for the record: (1) Option A, B or C; (2) with A, do *fruiting* (spores on the patch) and *stalks* (a corpse's spores) belong to the free pool, as listed, or to the ants pool (they act on ants walking through the spores); (3) should a colonised corpse's substance (`feed`) go to the free pool, as measured here, or the ants pool (it is a host's body)?

**Build, if A** (`ja-vlla.7`): `Evolution` gains a second `growth` and `spent` (saved only when above 0 so a one-pool save loads), a `budget` field on each upgrade, the drawer shows two meters, `progression.mjs` buys from both pools; the assays are P1 by the worst seed on 1–20 and the first-purchase times; goldens re-recorded and said so.
