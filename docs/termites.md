Termites are not ants. The ladder's third rung ([vision.md](vision.md), "Beyond ants: open") starts here: the owner chose termites after the leafcutter and before army ants (2026-11-12, PRD §64). The question: **what does a termite worker sense and do, and how much of the game's ant-shaped colony survives the change of order?** The answer is the measure of tier 3 ([species.md](species.md)).

## 1. Nests: tunnels to separate food, or a mound built by humidity

- Three nest types: one-piece (dampwood *Zootermopsis* lives inside its log), separate-piece (*Reticulitermes*, *Coptotermes* tunnel through soil to wood), and mounds (*Macrotermes*) (from memory; not fetched).
- *Macrotermes michaelseni*: relative humidity 92.6 ± 0.44 % in the mound's conduits and 94.2 ± 0.29 % at its apex. At an active build 93.5 % in the troughs, 89.4 % at the rim, 61.5 % 0.5 cm above; a 0.8 m/s wind drops the rim to 70.6 %. In the lab, soil moved in 4 h was 6.83 ± 2.76 g at 80 % RH against 4.86 ± 1.66 g at 40 %; in the field, 77.3 g sheltered against 41.2 g exposed in 12 h. Proposed rule: deposit at the edge of the humid zone (proposed, not measured). CO₂ not tested (Bardunias et al. 2020, *Proc R Soc B* 287:20200894, PMC7423481), **full text**.
- *Coptotermes gestroi* deposit where the surface curves most (pillar tips of about 0.4 cm radius, the arena's edge), the dominant cue in 7 of 11 runs; depositing is about ten times likelier than collecting at a site; groups of 50 workers and 5 soldiers. The authors exclude a cement pheromone for early site choice (Facchini et al. 2024, *eLife* 12:RP86843, PMC11006414), **full text**. Worked soil is an arrestant (Petersen et al. 2015), and building follows excavation and aggregation (Green et al. 2017), both **unverified**; no cement pheromone has been identified.
- *Reticulitermes flavipes* foraging territories up to 71 m across and 2,361 m²; *Coptotermes formosanus* 83–1,634 m² (13 New Orleans colonies), 11.5–56.8 m from the nest in China. **Unverified** (abstracts).

**In the game:** the dug curtain and its tunnels are closer to a subterranean termite's galleries than to anything else the game could add, so a **separate-piece termite keeps the world**: tunnels through soil to food items. The mound is a different nest kind (`ja-fnd.5.5`), and humidity a **new sense** (a world field: moisture near open air, low; deep, high; vision row below), the deposit rule a policy answer over it. The first build is the separate-piece termite with **no mound**; humidity-driven building is a later bead.

## 2. A king and queen, replaced by many neotenics that the queen holds back

- *Reticulitermes speratus*: female neotenics suppress new female neotenics (F₂,₂₆ = 64.83, P < 0.0001), males are unaffected; it works through a double mesh, so the signal is volatile: n-butyl-n-butyrate and 2-methyl-1-butanol at 2.14:1, emitted by the queens and by their eggs. A synthetic 2:1 blend suppresses, neither compound alone does; 100 eggs a day suppress, 20 do not (P = 0.34). Two field colonies held 208 and 103 female neotenics with one king; field colonies reach 500,000 (Matsuura et al. 2010, *PNAS* 107:12963, PMC2919916), **full text**.
- *R. flavipes* in the south-eastern USA: about 75 % of colonies are simple families (one pair), about 20 % extended (with neotenics), about 2 % mixed (Vargo & Husseneder 2009, from memory, via citing papers). **Unverified.**

**In the game:** the royal pair is a nest structure: **two** reproductives, one of them male, and replacement reproductives raised from workers or nymphs when the signal fades. The signal is the queen-pheromone row the game already has ([new-queens §2](new-queens.md#2-the-queen-holds-it-back)), now with eggs as a source: a sensed level, with the same "too little → rear replacements" rule. The structure (a king, neotenics, `queens[]`) is tier 3 (`ja-fnd.5.5`, `ja-8fd.1`).

## 3. Castes by development, not by age

- Soldiers hold back soldiers: soldier head extract alone does nothing to *R. flavipes* workers, but with juvenile hormone III it raises presoldier production; the main compounds are γ-cadinene and γ-cadinenal (Tarver et al. 2010, *BMC Mol Biol* 11:28, PMC2873311). **Unverified** (abstract).
- Soldier shares: *C. formosanus* about 10 % (4.7–5.0 % among foragers; Haverty 1977, **unverified**); *R. flavipes* field colonies 4–5 % (stated in Sun et al. 2013, full text); *Macrotermes gilvus* larvae 42.6 %, workers 42.2 %, soldiers 14.2 % (**unverified**).

**In the game:** caste is set by development, by sensed signals (soldier primer pheromone, juvenile hormone), not by age bands. In game terms: a moulting worker becomes a soldier with a chance that falls as soldier signal rises (body, a sensed level), and a soldier's actions are the policy's over a situation with a `caste` field (as the leafcutter's, [leafcutter §3](leafcutter.md#3-size-sets-the-task-minims-medias-majors)). The age bands (`band()`) do not apply: `youngShare`/`middleShare` set to put every worker in one band, and caste carried by a per-ant array. Tier 2 for the field, tier 3 for the moult.

## 4. Signals: a presence-only trail, and alarm by drumming relayed through the ground

- The trail pheromone is (3Z,6Z,8E)-dodecatrien-1-ol from the sternal gland. In *R. hesperus* the threshold is 0.01–0.1 fg/cm, the best about 10 fg/cm, and above 10 pg/cm it repels; workers did **not** follow gradients: they respond to its presence, not its strength (Saran et al. 2007, *J Chem Ecol* 33:369). **Unverified** (abstract).
- *Macrotermes* soldiers drum the substrate in pulse trains of 10–20 Hz, detectable only about 40 cm away, so the signal travels by relay (soldiers drum again); soldiers run toward the source and workers retreat (Hager & Kirchner 2013, *J Exp Biol* 216:3249; Röhrig, Kirchner & Leuthold 1999, *Insectes Soc* 46:71). **Unverified** (abstracts).
- *Constrictotermes cyphergaster*: the first defender reached a breach after 31 ± 1.8 s (5–60 s); up to 78 soldiers came against 2 workers; the soldiers' frontal-gland alarm is α-pinene, myrcene and (E)-β-ocimene at 100:88:1; running speed 3.95 mm/s at 2 gland-equivalents and 8.99 at 6; drumming in bursts of 2–7 beats (Cristaldo et al. 2015, *Biol Open* 4:1649, PMC4736033), **full text**.
- Cuticular hydrocarbons carry colony recognition; no single compound explains aggression; heneicosane is a royal recognition pheromone in *R. flavipes* (**unverified**).

**In the game:** the food trail is the generic channel with a **binary** read (present or not) for this species: **data** on the sensor (a reading capped at "faint"). Alarm is the vibration row of the sense table, **relayed**: an ant that feels drumming above a threshold drums too. That relay is a response hook (`raiseAlarm` on a vibration channel), with soldiers toward and workers away as the policy's answers by caste. Tier 2 (a vibration channel, `ja-fnd.5.11`).

## 5. The dead: eaten fresh, buried old, and the switch is smelled

- *R. flavipes* removes nestmate corpses within minutes (usually under 15 min), and how long the corpse has been dead (0–24 h) made no difference. Corpses of the related *R. virginicus* had soil on them within 10 min and were buried by 19.9 workers and 10.7 soldiers per observation, against 1.4 workers for a nestmate; alarm: 36.75 vibrations in 15 min against 1.75 (Sun, Haynes & Zhou 2013, *Sci Rep* 3:1650, PMC3629736), **full text**.
- *R. flavipes*: an early death cue (3-octanone and 3-octanol) brings retrieval and cannibalism; later decomposition cues switch workers to burial at about 32–64 h (Sun et al. 2017, *Funct Ecol* 31:697). **Unverified** (abstract).
- *C. formosanus* and *R. speratus* eat only fresh corpses (*C. formosanus* ate 51.7 % of maimed live termites and under 40 % of fresh corpses by 48 h; *R. speratus* under 20 %); carcasses three or more days old are walled off with sand (*R. speratus* buried 70–80 % by 48 h); recruitment took 5 min (*C. formosanus*) and 15 min (*R. speratus*). Higher termites (*Microcerotermes*) mostly ignore corpses or block the tube (Neoh et al. 2012, *PLoS ONE* 7:e36375, PMC3338677), **full text**.

**In the game:** **corpse odour** has been on vision.md's "not yet researched" line; this is its research for termites. Two odours on a corpse, fresh and decayed, sensed by contact; the policy answers eat (fresh) or bury in place (old), and a foreign corpse draws a crowd. `undertakes: boolean` becomes **what a species does with a corpse, by its odour**: carry to the midden (generic), eat, bury in place, ignore. Tier 2.

## 6. Against *Metarhizium*: groom, then eat the sick, then bury the dead

- *R. flavipes* given *M. anisopliae* (10⁸ conidia/ml) in groups of 48: grooming peaks 12 h after exposure; cannibalism appears from about 14.5–15 h and replaces grooming by 20 h; victims are eaten alive, starting with biting; burial seen in 4 plates (15–20 h); victims are immobile by 20 h and die at 24–48 h; the trigger is unknown (Davis et al. 2018, *Sci Rep* 8:14433, PMC6158180), **full text**.
- *C. formosanus* in groups of 50 over 11 days at 17 doses: mortality from 5.7 % (controls) to 95.7 %, LD50 about 6.3 × 10⁴ conidia/g of sand. Cannibalism handles the dead up to about 15 % mortality; above that burial takes over, peaking at 75–80 %; sporulating corpses among the living appear only above about 75 % mortality; 85.9 % of unburied corpses sporulated, buried ones sporulated in 2–3 days but sealed off (Chouvenc & Su 2012, *PLoS ONE* 7:e34484, PMC3314638), **full text**. No successful field biocontrol of termites with *Metarhizium* in about 50 years (same paper).
- *Zootermopsis angusticollis*: naive termites paired with immunised nestmates were 34.2 % less susceptible (P = 0.027) (Traniello, Rosengaus & Savoie 2002, *PNAS* 99:6838, PMC124490), **full text**. Alone, LT50 3.0–4.8 days; in groups of 10–25, 5.6–9.1 days (Rosengaus et al. 1998, **unverified**). Exposed termites give a vibratory alarm and nestmates move away; spores or odour alone do not trigger it; nothing below 10⁷ spores/ml (Rosengaus et al. 1999, **unverified**).
- *R. flavipes*: oscillation bursts of 2–7 s; one worker alone gives none, twelve give 115; alarm tracks aggregation (r = 0.513) and allogrooming (r = 0.502), and is weaker for the deadlier strain (r = −0.617); a sporulating corpse drew 105 bursts against 26 for a control corpse (Bulmer, Franco & Fields 2019, *Insects* 10:240, PMC6723859), **full text**.
- *C. formosanus*: highly virulent isolates are removed 2–10 times faster than weak ones; odour aversion to one *M. anisopliae* isolate starts at about 10⁵ conidia/ml; LD50 rises with group size (under 9.2 × 10² alone, 1.8 × 10⁴ in 5, 6.9 × 10⁴ in 10) (Yanagawa et al. 2012, *PLoS ONE* 7:e47412, PMC3471821), **full text**.
- **Contrary:** sick *R. flavipes* workers send no signal and move into the densest part of the colony (Moran et al. 2022, *Front Ecol Evol* 10:840223, **unverified**, abstract).

**In the game:** the strongest counterplay any host has, in three stages the policy can choose over sensed cues: **groom** on the fungus's odour (12 h), **eat** the moribund (from 15 h), **bury** the dead in place (overwhelmed past about 15 % dead). Sporulating corpses reach the living only once the colony is overwhelmed (about 75 %): a **capacity threshold**, which is the fungus's real path to winning here. Each stage reads a sensed cue (sickness cues, corpse odour), not a timer.

## 7. Between colonies: fusion is possible, and diet shapes aggression

- *R. flavipes*: 8 of 354 field colonies (2.3 %) were fusions, 6 of the 8 of unrelated families; in the lab 27 of 28 pairings of colonies merged without aggression (DeHeer & Vargo 2008, *Insectes Soc*, doi:10.1007/s00040-008-0999-0). **Unverified** (abstract; which of the two studies the research found holds which figure was not checked against the papers).
- *C. formosanus*: 13 of 40 colony pairs fought in 24-hour dish tests and 7 of 9 in 14-day arenas; aggression was not tied to relatedness and was lost within three months on a shared wood diet (Cornelius & Osbrink 2003, *Environ Entomol* 32:1002). **Unverified** (abstract).

**In the game:** the game's recognition odour and tolerance carry this as **data** (wide tolerance for *Reticulitermes*), and a diet-borne odour that converges is exactly the fungus's odour lever working through food: **design**, but with a basis.

## The mechanic

The termite is tier 3: a different nest kind, a royal pair and development-set castes. Cheapest first; "off" is the generic ant.

| Cue (sensed) | Sense row | Response | Local rule | Off | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trail present or not | trail pheromones | follow | binary read of the food channel | graded read | 1 (data) |
| Own caste (worker, soldier) | own body | the mode lane's situation (caste field) | soldiers answer alarm, workers dig and forage | one caste | 2 (`ja-fnd.5.10`) |
| Drumming felt | vibration | relay; soldiers toward, workers away | drum again above a threshold | none | 2 (`ja-fnd.5.11`) |
| Fungal odour / sickness on a nestmate | **sickness cues** | groom | as [lasius-neglectus](lasius-neglectus.md) §3 | generic grooming | 2 |
| A moribund nestmate | **sickness cues** | eat it | eat a nestmate whose sickness passes a high threshold | none | 2 |
| Corpse, fresh or decayed | **new: corpse odour** | eat (fresh), bury in place (old) | by the odour on the corpse | carry to the midden | 2 |
| Queen and egg volatile level | queen pheromone | rear neotenics when it fades | the existing threshold rule | one queen | 3 (`ja-fnd.5.5`, `ja-8fd.1`) |
| Soldier signal | **design** (a primer) | moult to soldier | chance falls with the soldier level | none | 3 |
| Humidity at a build face | **new: humidity** | deposit at the humid zone's edge | the proposed Bardunias rule | none | 3 (a mound, later) |

**Situation and lanes.** A termite lane of its own: modes forage, follow, return, defend; a caste field; no stance lane (no raids). Its words are new throughout ("termite", "soldier", "drumming").

**What it forces on the code.** Everything tier 3: the nest kind (`ja-fnd.5.5`) owning a royal pair and neotenics; the tick split (`ja-fnd.4.4`) so a soldier's and a worker's steering, eating and burial are separate steps, not branches of the ant's 1,200-line body; per-species schemas and channels (`ja-fnd.5.10`, `ja-fnd.5.11`); age bands that do not apply. It does **not** force a new world: a separate-piece termite lives in the dug curtain.

**Assays** (land first). T1: **the staged defence**: grooming peaks before cannibalism, which gives way to burial (Davis et al. 2018: 12 h, 15 h, 20 h, scaled). T2: **the capacity threshold**: sporulating corpses among the living only above about 75 % dead (Chouvenc & Su 2012). T3: **group protection**: LD50 rises with group size (Yanagawa et al. 2012). T4: **royal replacement**: neotenics appear once the queen signal falls (Matsuura et al. 2010).

**Cost (estimated from request size; not measured).** A lane with the generic fields' sizes plus `caste` (×2), minus stance: ~240 situations a game at most, ~$0.15/h while new, $0 cached; no stance lane (saves its ~$0.03/h, PRD §54.5). Its legend is new text throughout: the live check is a full new lane (~$0.01–0.02).

## Play

- **See:** a different animal: pale workers in the tunnels, soldiers with big heads at a breach, waves of drumming that pass through the soil (drawn as ripples), the sick eaten and the dead walled off with soil. Learn: termites are not ants; the staged defence.
- **Choose:** the fungus's whole game changes: grooming and eating are where it spreads (grooming moves spores; cannibalism of the moribund, if the fungus times its own growth past the window, feeds it in); burial seals it. Its levers on perception (masking its odour; the alarm it triggers) and its timing are the choice.
- **Counterplay and progression:** the hardest host in the literature (no field biocontrol in about 50 years): a late rung, or a scenario of its own.
- **Cost:** above; cached games spend nothing.
- **Risks:** an **unwinnable host** (the capacity threshold must be reachable on some seeds: measure time to a quarter); **invisible drumming** (must be drawn); **scope** (this is tier 3 and a new animal: the most code of any candidate).
- **Owner decisions needed:** see Open questions.
